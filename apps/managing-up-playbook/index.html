Core Framework

Six Principles of Managing Up

Distilled from centuries of historical wisdom, these principles form the foundation of effective upward influence.

🎭

Preserve Their Dignity

Like Michelangelo with the nose, let others believe improvement was their idea. Never make a superior feel foolish, even when they are wrong. The goal is the right outcome, not credit.

🧭

Speak Their Language

Franklin's "humble opinion" technique worked because he framed his ideas in terms his audience valued. Translate your expertise into the concerns of those above you.

⌛

Master Your Timing

Lincoln's unsent letters show that the right response at the wrong time is the wrong response. Learn when to act, when to wait, and when to let silence speak.

♟️

Think Three Moves Ahead

Like Zhuge Liang's empty fort, strategic positioning means arranging circumstances so that the desired outcome appears to be the natural — and only — choice.

🤝

Build Quiet Alliances

Eleanor Roosevelt transformed influence without authority into lasting institutional change. Build your network before you need it. Make allies, not adversaries.

🌊

Bend, Don't Break

Galileo, Scheherazade, and Hatshepsut all survived hostile power structures by adapting their approach without abandoning their core purpose. Flexibility preserves integrity.
