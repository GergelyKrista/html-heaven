Total War: Warhammer III · Skarbrand the Exiled · Beginner-friendly

Titlecard

☣Overview

The Exiles of Khorne are a roaming melee horde led by Skarbrand the Exiled, a fallen Bloodthirster doomed to drown his shame in blood. They have one job: kill, raze, repeat. No magic, no diplomacy, almost no ranged — just the strongest melee infantry in the game and a lord who alone can break a battle line.

Why this is a great beginner faction

  • One clear win condition: fight, win, raze, repeat. No book-keeping, no diplomacy puzzles, no spell schools.
  • Skarbrand is one of the strongest Legendary Lords in the game. He can solo most early-game armies.
  • Razed regions auto-resettle for free as long as you hold one settlement in the province — logistics handled.
  • Bloodletting + Skull Throne reward what you already want to do: keep moving, keep killing.

The Khorne loop, in one sentence

Win battle → sack settlement (movement preserved) → raze it for skulls or a Blood Host → +15% march range → reach the next target before they can react. Everything in the faction — resources, tech, lord skills, even the auto-resettle rule — is tuned around keeping that wheel spinning.

☣Lore — Why Skarbrand is the Way He Is

To understand the Exiles you have to understand two things: the Blood God's appetite, and the worst possible mistake a daemon can make in front of him.

Khorne, the Blood God

Khorne is one of the four Chaos Gods. He is the god of war, murder, honor in battle, and rage — pure martial fury made cosmic. His followers do not pray; they kill. He is fed by violence regardless of who commits it — his favorite quote sums up his theology in eight words: "Blood for the Blood God. Skulls for the Skull Throne."

Khorne despises sorcery. He sees magic as the cowardly tool of weaklings who cannot win with steel and courage. That is why a Khorne army has zero spellcasters — this is canon, not an oversight. It is also why Khorne's hordes are usually at war with Tzeentch's followers above all others.

Skarbrand — the Greatest Bloodthirster, Fallen

Skarbrand was once the greatest of all Bloodthirsters, Khorne's most exalted Greater Daemon and the leader of his celestial host. He was Khorne's favorite, his hand on the battlefield, his judgement in war.

Then the Changer of Ways, Tzeentch, whispered into Skarbrand's pride. Convinced he was greater than his god, Skarbrand raised his axes and struck Khorne himself. Khorne's wrath was instant and total. He hurled Skarbrand from his throne, destroyed his wings, and stripped away every part of his personality — cunning, memory, joy, even hate — leaving only one thing: boundless, bottomless rage.

The detail that defines him in battle: Skarbrand is the only Bloodthirster who cannot fly. Khorne tore the wings from him. Every other Bloodthirster lord in the game has flight; Skarbrand walks. This is why his vulnerability to ranged fire matters so much.

The Exile

Cast out of the Blood God's favor, Skarbrand now wanders the mortal and immortal realms with the singular obsession of drowning his shame in such a tide of blood that Khorne cannot help but forgive him. He never can — the more he kills, the more he reminds Khorne of the betrayal — but the rage will not let him stop.

That endless self-flagellating slaughter is what you, the player, are pointing in a direction. The "Exiles of Khorne" are the warband that follows him through it: mortal Khorne worshippers, bound daemons of the lesser host, and the trail of skulls he leaves on every continent he crosses.

The three Khorne factions — where the Exiles fit

Total War: Warhammer III ships three playable Khorne factions. Knowing they exist helps you read the campaign:

  • Exiles of Khorne — Skarbrand the Exiled. The disgraced Bloodthirster's roaming horde. The faction this guide covers.
  • Blooded Wanderers — Skulltaker. A daemonic champion seeking the eight greatest skulls.
  • Challengers of Khorne — Arbaal the Undefeated. A mortal champion. Different mechanics (Khorne's Favour, Challenges of Khorne, no Unholy Manifestations).

These three factions do not get along well — the in-game text spells out that the −10 modifier with other Khorne factions is "mostly Skarbrand only." The Blood God's exile is treated as a threat to the legitimate champions still in his favour.

Why this matters mechanically: the lore explains every faction quirk. No magic (Khorne forbids it). No diplomacy (Khorne wants war, not allies; even other Khorne factions resent the exile). Replenish in foreign territory (he is supposed to keep moving). +15% march after razing (the rage drives him forward). Skulls as currency (literally what he offers Khorne as proof).

☣General Khorne Mechanics

These four systems apply to every Khorne faction in the game. Master them and you understand the race.

☣

Skulls

Khorne's secondary currency. Earned from every battle you fight (win or lose), from razing settlements, from Skull Pile points-of-interest on the map, and as passive trickle from certain buildings and cults.

Spent on: occupying settlements, researching technologies, and activating Skull Throne offerings.

⚔

Bloodletting

A per-army gauge that fills with consecutive battle victories and drains while the army is idle. Higher tiers grant escalating bonuses: upkeep reduction, replenishment, faction-wide growth, local Khorne corruption, cheaper global recruitment.

It punishes you for sitting still. Move every turn, fight every turn.

⚔

Blood Hosts

When you raze a settlement and pick the "Blood for the Blood God" option, a free temporary army spawns in the region. It can't recruit, can't replenish (except by devouring captives), has no upkeep, and does not count toward your supply-lines penalty.

After several turns it begins suffering attrition and eventually dies — winning battles delays this. Use it as a second wave: raze nearby villages, soak hits, reinforce a real army, or trade it for an enemy stack.

☣

The Skull Throne

A faction-level offering window. Spend a pile of skulls to unlock 10-turn buffs — e.g. +post-battle loot, +campaign movement, summon Khorne armies, army ability tokens.

Save a big offering for the turn you launch a major offensive, not for a quiet turn.

Three more rules that shape every Khorne campaign

Auto-resettlement of ruined regions

If you hold at least one settlement in a province, every other ruined region in that province will eventually colonize itself for free with a Khorne settler — the chance grows each turn. This is the rule that makes razing the default. You don't have to "choose between scorched earth and growth" — you get both, on a delay.

Limited diplomacy

Every non-Chaos faction has a hard-wired diplomatic penalty against you. Trade is impossible (Khorne has no tradeable resources). However, the Blood God isn't completely isolated — the wiki notes that pacts and even alliances with some other Chaos and Destruction factions are still possible. The default is war; treat any peace agreement as a tool to focus on a different front.

Innate magic resistance & faction abilities

Most Khorne units have Spell Resistance baked in (exceptions: Bloodbrute Behemoth and Chaos Warhounds). You also get three faction-level army abilities: Horn of Khorne, Relentless Rage, and Blade of Khorne, fueled by killing. Khorne also has Unholy Manifestations and Cults like other daemonic factions.

☣Exiles-Specific Mechanics

On top of the four general Khorne systems, the Exiles get a small set of unique faction effects that all pull in the same direction: be a roaming horde, not a stationary empire.

+15% Campaign Movement after razing

Razing a settlement isn't just a money/skull event — it extends your turn. You can often raze, then march out of the now-ruined province and engage another army or city in the same turn.

Replenishment in foreign territory

Most factions only replenish meaningfully in friendly land or under Khorne corruption. The Exiles heal almost everywhere, so a stack that loses a third of its strength can be combat-ready again in 2–3 turns deep in enemy land.

−10 with other Khorne factions

A flavor mechanic with bite: even Khornate kin treat the Exiles as outcasts. You will probably end up at war with Arbaal, Valkia, and the Bloodthirster of Skulls' faction at some point. Don't expect a Khorne alliance bloc.

How these stack in practice

Compounding effect: Skarbrand can plunge into foreign land, raze a city, gain skulls + a Blood Host + 15% extra movement, then march again to a second target while replenishing in "enemy" territory. By the time the target's reinforcements arrive, Skarbrand's army is healed and ready to fight again. This is the core engine of the campaign.

Skarbrand's lord-line bonuses

  • Slaughter & Carnage: Skarbrand's weapon strength climbs with every kill he makes in a battle. By mid-fight he is hitting harder than anything in his price bracket.
  • Wrathful Reaper: active ability, locks him on a target unit and ignores distractions until that unit is destroyed.
  • Rage Embodied: AoE active ability, sends nearby friendly units into a damage-buffed frenzy.
  • Charge bonus & speed: for a melee monster, Skarbrand is fast. He picks his fights.

☣Skarbrand the Exiled

Skarbrand the Exiled
Greater Daemon of Khorne · Fallen Bloodthirster · Legendary Lord

Arguably the strongest pure-melee Legendary Lord in the game. Single-handedly wins early battles, scales into late game, and is the reason this faction is rated beginner-friendly.

Combat profile

Monster Anti-Large Armor-Piercing Flaming Causes Terror Causes Fear Encourage Siege Attacker No flight Single Entity
  • Towering stats: 9448 HP, 90 armor (67.5% reduction), Leadership 85, Speed 80, Charge Bonus 65, Weapon Strength 620 (185 base + 435 AP).
  • Physical Resistance +20%, Spell Resistance +35% — he shrugs off both magic and ordinary attacks.
  • Resilience: immune to Chaos Wastes, Chaos corruption, and Snow attrition — great for late-game raiding into hostile climates.
  • Siege Attacker: can hit gates and walls without towers or rams — instant siege launches.
  • Slaughter & Carnage (item): his weapon damage scales with every kill in the current battle. He snowballs.
  • Active abilities: Bellow of Endless Fury, Wrathful Reaper (single-target obsession), Rage Embodied (AoE frenzy on allies); plus Rage Incarnate on the campaign map.

Weaknesses to respect

The two ways to lose Skarbrand

  • Massed missiles & artillery. He's a huge single entity with no flight and no shield. Empire Great Cannons, Dwarf Organ Guns, Skaven Ratling Guns, anything that focuses him will eat his health bar.
  • Melee Defence is only 50. His armor and resists keep him alive, but he does take return hits — lots of cheap chaff swarming him still chips him down.
Don't pull him out at 50% HP — that's when he gets stronger. His passive Fuelled by Rage triggers below 50% health for a +25% damage boost on top of his already monstrous output. Pulling him is correct only if focused artillery / ranged stacks are killing him, not just because the bar is red.

Lord Effects (army-wide)

  • Bloodthirster and Soul Grinder units in his army get the "Gorefeast" passive ability.
  • −20% upkeep for Bloodthirsters and Soul Grinders in his army.
  • +30% Skulls gained from kills (Skarbrand's army).
  • Grants the "Rage Embodied" ability army-wide.
Build implication: Bloodthirsters and Soul Grinders are objectively cheaper and stronger in Skarbrand's army than in any second lord's. Run them in his stack; recruit cheaper anchor units (Chaos Warriors, Bloodletters) for your secondary lords.

Skill tree priorities (in order)

  1. Yellow / red line damage and survivability first. Anything that adds armor, weapon strength, or charge bonus to Skarbrand directly is points well spent.
  2. Blood Feast / Drinker of Blood — sustain in long battles and quest fights.
  3. Unholy Resilience — physical damage resistance is the single biggest mitigator of his ranged weakness.
  4. Lightning Strike as soon as available — lets him pick off armies before reinforcements arrive. Critical for an aggressor.
  5. Blue line campaign skills — campaign movement, replenishment, post-battle loot. Take these once Skarbrand is already a one-man army.
  6. Army leadership and unit-bonus buffs (red side) — helps your Bloodletters and Chaos Warriors avoid breaking when Skarbrand is busy elsewhere.
Mounts: Skarbrand has no mount choice — he is the mount. Don't hunt for one in the skill tree.

How to use him in battle

  1. Deploy him near the front line, on the side with the most enemy melee lords, heroes, monsters, or threatening frontline units.
  2. Open by charging the most threatening frontline single-entity target — trigger Wrathful Reaper. Use the charge bonus: cycle in, around, in early game; he hits hardest on the charge.
  3. Stay in the thick of the action. Tempting as it is to two-shot an enemy backline wizard, the wiki is explicit: don't send Skarbrand racing off to hunt backline lords. He needs continuous kills to scale Slaughter & Carnage and the campaign-only kill passives. Send Furies / Flesh Hounds / a flying Bloodthirster after backline targets instead.
  4. Drop Rage Embodied at the moment of breakthrough, next to your Bloodletters — it amplifies a rout, it doesn't open one.
  5. Don't pull him out the moment his health is yellow. Below 50% HP, Fuelled by Rage activates and he hits 25% harder. Pull only if specific ranged units are visibly chunking him.

☣Faction Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths

  • Best melee infantry in the game. Chaos Warriors of Khorne are arguably the best Tier-2 frontline anywhere; Bloodletters cut elite infantry apart.
  • Skarbrand wins early-game battles single-handed.
  • Outstanding monster roster: Minotaurs (anti-large), Bloodthirsters (best monster hunter), Soul Grinder (only monster with ranged), Chaos Spawn (cheap unbreakable disruption).
  • Anti-large saturation. Almost every unit in the roster has decent answers to enemy lords and monsters.
  • Free expansion. Auto-resettlement + Blood Hosts + razing-derived skulls = no logistics tax.
  • High mobility. +15% march after razing, foreign-territory replenishment, Lightning Strike on Skarbrand. You can blitz an entire province in 3–4 turns.
  • Skull Throne gives faction-wide power-spike windows when you need them.

Weaknesses

  • No spellcasters at all. No magic, ever. You have no answer to enemy magic except killing the caster — though most Khorne units do have innate Spell Resistance to mitigate damage.
  • Almost no ranged. Only the Skullcannon, the Soul Grinder, and the Marauder Horsemen. None of them are a true sustained gun line.
  • Poor against gunpowder & massed missiles. Empire, Dwarfs, Skaven, Cathay all force you to absorb a punishing approach phase.
  • Weak economy by building income. Buildings cap at low gold; income comes from sacking, not provinces.
  • No diplomacy. Negative relations with everyone, including other Khorne factions. No trade deals, near-impossible alliances.
  • Cavalry has low melee defense. Skullcrushers, Bloodcrushers, Gorebeast Chariots all need to cycle-charge; they melt if you leave them stuck in.
  • Bloodletting decays. If you stop fighting, your bonuses dry up — the faction is punishing if you try to play turtle.

☣Campaign Strategy

The Exiles play like a roaming horde even though they technically settle. Treat your home province as a refit base and your other armies as expendable wrecking balls.

Opening (Immortal Empires — Deff Gorge start)

Realms of Chaos players: your start is different — Skarbrand begins at Infernius in the Chaos Wastes, surrounded by jagged mountains and lava rivers, working through the Champions of Chaos / Realms of Chaos rifts campaign. The opening sequence below is for the more popular Immortal Empires campaign at Deff Gorge.
  1. Turn 1: confirm Skarbrand's army composition, head straight for the nearest Savage Orc settlement. Don't dawdle; the start is forgiving but Bloodletting starts ticking up only when you fight.
  2. First battles: lead with Skarbrand into orc Big 'Uns / Boyz. Cycle Chaos Warhounds onto enemy archers/shamans. Keep your infantry blob tight.
  3. First settlement: sack, then raze (Blood for the Blood God). You get gold, skulls, a Blood Host, and your full movement back. See the Settlements tab for the full rule.
  4. Found your first owned settlement only on a province capital in a region you intend to keep. Spend the skulls. Build basic recruitment up to Tier 2 fast.
  5. Recruit a second lord (Herald of Khorne or Exalted Bloodthirster) once your treasury can afford the army. Send the Blood Hosts as a screen ahead of him.
  6. By turn ~12 you should be running 2 main armies + 1–2 Blood Hosts. Pick a direction (typically north into Greenskin lands or Borderlands) and roll.

The mid-game pattern

1 home province + 2 armies + a roaming wedge. Hold one province properly (capital + 2 minor settlements) for recruitment income and Bloodletting bonuses. Keep your two main stacks moving in different directions, razing as they go. Let auto-resettlement fill the empty regions behind you.

Empire expansion principles

  • Hold province capitals, raze the rest. Capitals can build Tier-3+ chains; minor settlements in your home province auto-resettle for free anyway.
  • Don't try to hold a 5-province empire. You don't need the income (your income is sacking) and you can't garrison everything.
  • Keep moving. Idle armies drain Bloodletting and waste your economy — an army standing still is an army losing money.
  • Don't fight wars on two fronts unless one front is razed-and-fleeing. Always blitz one neighbor flat before turning to the next.
  • Use Blood Hosts as expendable scouts/raiders. They can't replenish; spend them.

When to hold the Skull Throne offering

The big Skull Throne offerings (post-battle loot, campaign movement, summon a Khorne army) are 10-turn buffs. Save them for the turn you start a major offensive — declaring war on a new region, pushing for a Realms of Chaos rift, or grinding a Chaos Wastes faction. Don't burn them in a quiet turn.

Endgame & victory

Both short and long victories want you sweeping the map; the engine is the same as the early game. The only adjustment is that mid-game you'll start running into ranged-heavy factions (Empire, Dwarfs, Cathay) where you'll want to recruit a second wave that includes Skullcannons and Soul Grinders for the approach phase.

☣Battle Strategy

Khorne battles look brainless. They are not. Without ranged or magic, your only tools are positioning and timing — where you fight, when you charge, and which enemy unit dies first.

Standard battle plan

  1. Deploy in a line, slightly oblique. Chaos Warriors with shields take the front, Bloodletters in the second rank or staggered to flank.
  2. Send Furies / Warhounds wide to swing around the enemy flank towards their archers and artillery. Kill the ranged units first — this is the single most important rule for a Khorne player.
  3. Charge with the line when the enemy is committed. Khorne infantry wants to be moving when it hits, not standing still.
  4. Skarbrand drives at the enemy frontline lord, monsters, or biggest threat — staying in the thick of the action. Don't peel him off to chase backline wizards; that breaks his kill-scaling.
  5. Cycle-charge cavalry. Ride in, hit a flank, ride out, reform, hit again. Skullcrushers parked in melee will die.
  6. Drop Rage Embodied at the moment of breakthrough — not at the start. You're amplifying a rout, not opening one.

Specific matchups

vs. Gunpowder line (Empire / Dwarfs)

Don't walk Skarbrand at handgunners. Send Chaos Furies, Warhounds, and ideally a flying Bloodthirster lord to dive the back line. Your front line absorbs one volley and then closes.

vs. Cavalry-heavy (Bretonnia / Vampire Counts)

Halberd Chaos Warriors and great-weapon Minotaurs anchor. Use Skullcrushers to counter-charge their charge. Skarbrand hunts their lord.

vs. Magic-heavy (Tzeentch / High Elves / Lizardmen)

You have no dispel, but most Khorne units have innate Spell Resistance. Send a flying Bloodthirster lord, Chaos Furies, or Flesh Hounds after the wizard — not Skarbrand. Skarbrand should stay engaged in the kill-stacking front line.

vs. Monster spam (Lizardmen / Beastmen / Khorne mirror)

You have the best anti-large roster in the game. Halberd Warriors, Bloodletters with great weapons, Minotaurs GW, Bloodthirster. You should win the trade.

vs. Ranged-heavy (Wood Elves / Skaven slings)

Your ugliest matchup. Bring 2–4 Skullcannons; advance behind your line; do not let battles last long. Forests are death — force open ground.

vs. Anti-armor swarms (Skaven Stormvermin / Goblins with spears)

Don't bother Skarbrand-tanking. Cycle-charge cavalry through them, finish with Bloodletters, and watch enemy leadership collapse from Terror.

Auto-resolve advice

Skarbrand's army auto-resolves better than it should against most early-game enemies because the AI weights his stats heavily. Use this to clear weak-but-numerous foes (Greenskins, Beastmen). Never auto-resolve against gunpowder or ranged-heavy armies — the auto-resolver gives the AI free hits on Skarbrand and you'll watch your health bar drop for no reason.

☣Raze, Sack, or Occupy?

This is the single most-asked Exiles question. The answer changes only by one variable: are you in your home province or somewhere else?

The three actions and what they do

Sack

Beat the garrison, take a one-time gold payout (3k–40k depending on tier and wealth), do not occupy, the city stays where it was.

  • Does not end your army's movement — can chain
  • Generates skulls
  • City survives, can be sacked again later
First action against any non-keeper city.

Raze

Burn the settlement to ruins. Two flavor options:

  • Skulls for the Skull Throne: bigger skull payout
  • Blood for the Blood God: replenish army + spawn a free Blood Host
  • Restores your full army movement (campaign loop!)
  • Auto-resettles for free if it's in your province
Default action after sacking, or any time you don't need the city.

Occupy

Costs skulls. You now own the city, garrison, build it up, defend it.

  • Ends your movement
  • Adds a war front you have to defend
  • Gives you a recruitment / building site
Only province capitals in your home cluster. Be picky.

The decision rule (memorize this)

Sack first — always. It costs no movement and gets you the gold. Then:
  • If it's a province capital in your home cluster → occupy.
  • If you need army healing or a Blood Host right now → raze: Blood for the Blood God.
  • If you're flush on health and want skulls → raze: Skulls for the Skull Throne.
  • If it's a minor settlement in your home province → raze it; it'll re-colonize for free.

Why "sack then raze" is not a bug

Sacking and razing are independent actions. The gold from sacking is unaffected by razing afterwards, and razing still gives you the full skulls + Blood Host bonus. Anyone who tells you to skip the sack is leaving 5k–40k gold on the table. The only exception is when sacking would cost movement you need elsewhere — but that almost never happens because sacking does not in fact end movement.

Which provinces should you actually keep?

  • Your starting capital region (Deff Gorge in IE) — always.
  • One adjacent province for redundancy and a recruitment buffer.
  • Province capitals only beyond that. Don't try to hold every minor settlement; auto-resettlement covers your home cluster automatically.
  • Avoid keeping cities surrounded by climates marked "Uninhabitable" for Daemons of Chaos — the public-order penalty is brutal.
Climate trap: Daemons of Chaos suffer in many climates. Before you commit to occupying a juicy distant capital, check the climate icon. If it's red (Uninhabitable), raze instead and let some other faction live there — you'll just bleed money fighting public order.

☣Diplomacy & Economy

Khorne plays both like an alien race. Your diplomacy screen is mostly cosmetic, and your economy is fed by your sword arm.

Diplomacy: limited, not useless

  • You have hard-coded relations penalties with every non-Chaos faction. Trade is impossible (no tradeable resources).
  • Other Khorne factions specifically dislike you — the −10 modifier is largely a Skarbrand thing, since you are an exiled traitor in Khorne's eyes.
  • However, the wiki is clear that pacts and even alliances with some other Chaos and Destruction factions are still possible. Don't dismiss the diplomacy screen entirely — check it for opportunistic NAPs.
  • Useful actions: Non-Aggression Pacts with backline Chaos/Destruction neighbors so you can focus a single front, and Joint War declarations when another Chaos power is already hammering a shared enemy.
  • Confederating other Khorne factions is occasionally possible deep in the campaign — great for absorbing units — but hard to set up because of the hated-exile modifier. Don't plan for it early.
  • Note: some Khorne tech actively debuffs your diplomatic relations — Khorne demands war, not peace.
Practical rule: open the diplomacy screen mostly for NAPs with backline Chaos/Destruction neighbors. Don't expect trade and don't waste turns courting Order factions.

Economy: how you actually make money

Khorne building income is intentionally crippled — expect ≤ 60 gold per income building, well below other races. Your treasury is fed from three sources, in order of importance:

  1. Sacking settlements. By far the biggest. A single sack is 3k–40k gold depending on the city's tier and wealth. Plan your route around sackable cities.
  2. Battle income. 5k–10k per significant battle (post-battle loot, plus "Skulls for Khorne!" sacrificing of captured enemies).
  3. Raze gold. Smaller payout than sacking, but compounds with skulls and Blood Host generation.

Building priorities for the cities you do keep

  • Tier-up the capital recruitment chain first — you need access to Tier-3 Bloodletters and Skullcrushers, fast.
  • Skull-generating buildings next — passive skull income lets you afford tech and Skull Throne offerings during quiet turns.
  • Then public order / growth buildings only as needed.
  • Income-only buildings are nearly worthless — don't waste slots on them unless you have nothing else to build.

Cash-flow troubleshooting

If your treasury is bleeding red:
  • You probably have an idle main army — get it moving and into a battle this turn.
  • Disband or merge a recruited army you can't keep paying. Don't bother disbanding Blood Hosts — they have zero upkeep and don't count toward supply lines, so they're free; just spend them in a fight before attrition kills them.
  • Raid-stance any army that has nothing to attack this turn — small income, useful when bridging.
  • Find the nearest sackable city and march for it; one sack solves most cash crises.

Public order & corruption

  • Khorne corruption automatically rises in your home province — spreads passively to neighbors.
  • Public order in occupied cities is helped by maintaining high Khorne corruption and by garrisoning a hero.
  • If a settlement is in revolt, let it — you get a free fight, more skulls, more Bloodletting. Free practice.

☣Technologies

Tech is paid in skulls, not gold. Early game you'll feel the cost; mid-game you'll have skulls to spare. Pick technologies that compound your existing strategy — razing, Blood Hosts, and your core melee units.

Priority order

  1. Skull-generation tech first. "Serial Killing" / "Skull Harvesting" / equivalents — more skulls per kill and per raze means everything else gets cheaper.
  2. Blood Host buffs. Anything that increases Blood Host strength, lifespan, or numbers compounds your razing income.
  3. Army-wide unit buffs — especially Bloodletter and Chaos Warrior bonuses (your two staple units). "Spread Rage" or any Khorne-corruption-on-attack tech is excellent.
  4. Recruitment cost & speed reductions. Khorne armies are expensive; any % off makes second and third stacks viable.
  5. Replenishment / movement — campaign-layer mobility is force multiplier #1 for the Exiles.
  6. Late-game elite buffs — Bloodthirster, Skullcrusher, Skullcannon-targeted techs come last.

What to skip (or de-prioritize)

  • Building-income tech — you don't have an income economy; the percentage of nothing is nothing.
  • Defensive / siege-defender tech — you're not a defender. You're the one besieging.
Some Khorne technologies actively debuff your diplomatic relations. The Blood God isn't interested in peace treaties. If you are trying to maintain a NAP with a Chaos neighbor, watch which techs you unlock — some will sour the relationship.

Tech & Skull Throne — the same wallet

Skulls are spent on three things: occupying settlements, researching tech, and Skull Throne offerings. Don't drain your skull pile dry on tech in a turn you might want to occupy a juicy capital next turn. Plan ~1–2 turns ahead.

What "good tech" looks like in practice

  • By turn 25 you should have +1 to +2 Blood Hosts per raze, cheaper recruitment, and at least one major skull-generation buff.
  • By turn 50 your stacks should be running with missile resistance / replenishment tech and your Bloodthirsters / Skullcannons should have a buff applied.
  • By turn 75+ you're closing out the tree on quality-of-life: leadership, public order, and chasing-down-runaways.
The compounding insight: the Khorne tech tree is designed so the things you do anyway (kill, raze, spawn Blood Hosts) get permanent buffs. There are no "optimal off-meta" lines — the obvious-looking choices are the right ones. If a tech buffs killing, take it.
