The Northern Provinces — New Player's Guide

Total War: Warhammer III · Miao Ying, the Storm Dragon · Beginner-friendly

☯Overview

The Northern Provinces are the militant northern wing of Grand Cathay, ruled by Miao Ying, the Storm Dragon — first-born of the Celestial Dragon Emperor and Moon Empress, charged with defending the Great Bastion against the Hobgoblin Khanate, Norscan reavers, Kurgan Warbands, Chaos Dwarfs, and any other horror that crawls south from the Eastern Steppes. Miao Ying is a dragon-shapeshifting wizard, the Bastion is a wall that has never fallen entirely, and your job is to make sure it never does on your watch.

Why The Northern Provinces is a great campaign to learn Cathay

  • The Great Bastion is the headline mechanic: well-upgraded gates usually defeat Kurgan Warbands by themselves, and −20% construction cost on Grand Bastion buildings is the best wall-economy in any Cathay faction.
  • Miao Ying is one of the most well-rounded Legendary Lords in the game — caster, dragon-form bruiser, and Harmony Amplifier all in one.
  • Faction-wide +10 leadership vs. Daemons / Warriors of Chaos / Chaos Dwarfs / Norsca — the matchups the campaign forces on you are exactly the matchups the faction is built for.
  • Faction-wide +20% ammunition for missile units means your gunline never runs dry.
  • The Ivory Road and the Wu-Xing Compass give you economic and magical levers no other race has.

☯Lore — The Storm Dragon and Her Wall

To understand the Northern Provinces you have to understand three things: who ultimately rules Cathay, who Miao Ying is in that family, and what the Great Bastion was built to keep out.

Grand Cathay — the Empire of the Celestial Dragon

Grand Cathay is the largest empire in the Warhammer World, occupying most of the Far East. It is ruled by the Celestial Dragon Emperor and his consort the Moon Empress — immortal dragons who can shapeshift into human form — from the Imperial Court. In practice, the day-to-day governing of the empire falls to their nine children, who rule individual provinces. Of the nine, four have been lost to time and enmity; five remain.

Cathay's people are mostly humans, with smaller populations of Monkey-Warriors, and trading cities that host Tilean, Estalian, Imperial, High Elf, and Dwarf foreigners. The empire is a power of Order, a staunch opponent of Chaos — but its position in the Far East makes it isolated, thousands of miles from any natural ally.

Miao Ying — the Storm Dragon, eldest child of the Emperor

Miao Ying is "the favoured child of the Celestial Emperor, revered defender of the Great Bastion and imperious majesty incarnate." She is the first-born, given the honour of ruling the Northern Provinces and charged with Cathay's defence as commander of the Great Bastion. She has held this position for centuries.

She is famously cold and aloof. Her armies are almost constantly at war, fighting the predations of Chaos that grow against the Bastion. Her father entrusts her with important tasks — some of which put her at odds with her siblings.

The detail that defines her in battle: Miao Ying is both a wizard and a melee monster. She fights in two forms: Standard (humanoid, armoured, sword-and-spell caster with full Wizard kit) and Dragon Form (a transformation ability that turns her into a single-entity flying dragon). Both forms cast spells. The Dragon form is faster and devastating in single-entity combat; the Standard form is a more flexible front-line caster.

The Great Bastion

The most notable feature of the Northern Provinces is the Great Bastion — a huge, ancient fortification stretching along most of Cathay's northern border. It guards against the Hobgoblin Khanate, the forces of Chaos, and other attackers from the Eastern Steppes. Along its length lie gates, guarded by mighty fortresses. The Great Bastion has a mysterious, magical connection to the Dragon Emperor himself. On rare occasions it has been breached — and when it has, the forces of Chaos have cut a bloody swath into the heartland of Cathay — but it has never fallen entirely.

Nan-Gau — the City of Smoke

In the northwest of the Northern Provinces lies Nan-Gau, the city of smoke — countless forges and workshops producing arms and machines for Cathay's armies, including the famous Sky-junks. Because of Nan-Gau's importance to the empire, its Lords are granted more independence than most. Your starting capital and a defining piece of the campaign's identity.

The four playable Cathay factions — where the Northern fits

Cathay ships three vanilla playable factions, plus the DLC Tiger Warriors:

  • The Northern Provinces — Miao Ying, the Storm Dragon. The Bastion guardians. The faction this guide covers.
  • The Western Provinces — Zhao Ming, the Iron Dragon. Guards the city of Shang-Yang in the Warpstone Desert and the Ivory Road westward.
  • The Jade Court — Yuan Bo, the Jade Dragon. Rules from Shang Wu; chief bureaucrat and Administrator of the Realm.
  • (DLC) Bhashiva & the Tiger Warriors — Bhashiva, the White Tiger.

There are two siblings not playable: Yin-Yin the Sea Dragon (Eastern Provinces) and Li Dao the Fire Dragon (Southern Provinces). The dragon family doesn't always live in harmony — jealous competition between siblings is part of the campaign flavour.

Why this matters mechanically: the lore is the mechanics. The Bastion is real terrain you maintain. Harmony — the balance of Yin and Yang — is the worldview of Cathay that the game expresses as a meter you steer. The Wu-Xing Compass is the Imperial influence over the Winds of Magic. Caravans to the Old World on the Ivory Road are the same caravans Zhao Ming's faction wrote into history. The flavour is the rulebook.

☯General Cathay Mechanics

These five systems apply to every Grand Cathay faction. Master them and you understand the race.

☯

Harmony (Yin / Yang)

Cathay's philosophical core. Each action you take pushes a Harmony meter toward either Yin or Yang. The greatest faction-wide bonuses come from keeping the meter balanced in the centre; extreme tilt to either pole gives you partial bonuses but loses the harmony bonus.

It is, mechanically, similar to Bretonnia's Peasant Economy — balance two resources to maximise output without penalties.

✨

The Wu-Xing Compass

A magical campaign-map device that directs the Winds of Magic across the land. Pointing the Compass at a faction-wide buff (military, economic, supply) gives you a powerful temporary effect across every region you own.

Pair Compass directions with Harmony alignment for compounding effects — this is one of the strategic levers other races simply do not have.

🌂

The Ivory Road

Cathay's trade-caravan campaign system. Your factions can send trade caravans west along the Ivory Road to the Old World — income, plus en-route dilemmas that may enhance the rewards or destroy the caravan entirely.

Equip caravans with sturdy infantry and anti-large units — especially against Ogre threats. Defeating an Ogre Lord during a caravan run grants meaningful loot.

🎞

The Great Bastion

The mighty fortification along Cathay's northern border. It will come under attack; building it up confers great benefits. Each gate has its own threat bar, garrison, and defensive infrastructure that you upgrade over time.

For the Northern Provinces specifically, the Bastion is your campaign — see the Great Bastion tab for the deep dive.

Battle Harmony — Yin and Yang on the battlefield

Yin units (typically ranged)

When near a Yang-aligned ally, Yin units gain a reload speed buff.

Examples: Crane Gunners, Iron Hail Gunners, Jade Warrior Crossbowmen, Astromancers. Place these behind or beside your Yang line.

Yang units (typically melee)

When near a Yin-aligned ally, Yang units gain a melee defence buff.

Examples: Peasant Long Spearmen, Jade Warriors, Celestial Dragon Guard, Jade Lancers. Anchor them in front of or alongside your Yin shooters.

The fundamental Cathay battle rule: never deploy a Yin unit out of supporting range of a Yang unit (or vice versa). A Crane Gunner at half buff is a Crane Gunner at half value. Diverse, well-supported formations are the entire design philosophy of the army.

Mastery of the Elemental Winds — the caster bonus

Cathay's casters have a unique boost based on caster count in the same army. Each caster after the first adds +15% damage / buff power, up to a maximum of +100%:

Casters in armyBonusPractical effect
1+0%Baseline magic
2 (e.g. Lord + Hero)+15%Slight scaling
3+30%Real impact spike
4+45%Caster-doomstack territory
5+60%Spells dominate the battle
7++100% (cap)Magic-army endgame

Eight lores of magic

Cathay's casters can access Lore of Beasts, Lore of Heavens, Lore of Life, Lore of Light, Lore of Metal, Lore of Shadows, Lore of Yang, and Lore of Yin — one of the broadest magic lists in the game. Different lord/hero types unlock different lores.

Roster identity

"Legions of weaker, cheaper human troops backed up by a diverse roster of stronger elite troops, flying cavalry, monsters, and war machines/artillery. A defensive, less mobile army vulnerable to flanking." Build for set-piece battles, not chase-the-enemy-down.

☯The Northern Provinces — Faction-Specific Effects

On top of the general Cathay systems, Miao Ying's faction gets a tight bundle of effects that all push toward one identity: defend the Great Bastion, hold a missile-heavy line, and crush Chaos when it finally throws itself at your wall.

Faction effects (always-on)

Defensive Supplies: +300 (all regions)

Defensive Supplies are the regional resource that fuels Bastion upgrades and garrison reinforcements. +300 across every region you own means your Bastion gates and provincial defences capitalise faster than any other Cathay faction.

−20% Grand Bastion construction cost

A factionwide discount on every Grand Bastion building chain. Stack with Defensive Supplies and you have the cheapest, fastest-growing Bastion upgrade economy in Cathay.

Corruption: −2

A baseline corruption suppression in every region. Combined with Cathay's Untainted default, your provinces shed Chaos / Skaven / Vampire corruption automatically — no anti-corruption building chains required.

+10 Leadership vs. Chaos & Norsca

A flat +10 leadership when fighting Daemons of Chaos, Warriors of Chaos, Chaos Dwarfs, and Norsca. The exact factions you'll be fighting at the Bastion. Your Peasant Long Spearmen suddenly hold the line like Celestial Dragon Guard.

+20% Ammunition for missile units

Every missile unit in your faction has 20% more ammunition. Crane Gunners, Iron Hail Gunners, Jade Warrior Crossbowmen, Sky-junks — all run longer in protracted defensive battles.

Diplomatic traits: Protective, Bastion Defender, Caravan Master

Other factions read you as a defensive, trade-friendly Bastion power. Bastion Defender in particular is a reputation hook with Cathayan minor factions.

Starting state (Immortal Empires)

  • Starting at war: Rebel Lords of Nan-Yang (your immediate target) and Kurgan Warband (intermittent Bastion-attackers).
  • Starting NAP: Imperial Wardens — a friendly minor Cathay faction.

Starting state (Realm of Chaos)

  • Starting at war: Rebel Lords of Nan-Yan, Dissenter Lords of Jinshen, Kurgan Warband, Sarthorael's Watchers, Dreaded Wo, and Khazag.
  • Starting NAP: Celestial Loyalists.

How these stack in practice

Compounding effect: a Kurgan Warband approaches the Bastion. Your gate has been upgraded for half-price (−20%) and stocked with Defensive Supplies (+300/region). The garrison's Peasant Long Spearmen hold +10 leadership against Chaos and won't break. Crane Gunners overhead have +20% ammunition; their fire never falters. Miao Ying flies in from another gate — transforms into the Storm Dragon — and the Warband is dead before they breach. The faction is the wall and the dragon that protects the wall.

Miao Ying's lord-line bonuses

  • Supreme Matriarch (personal trait): "I bring the storm winds!"
  • Upkeep: −50% for Missile Infantry units in her army.
  • Recruit rank: +2 for Iron Hail Gunners, Crane Gunners, and Nan-Gau Grenadiers in her army.
  • Missile strength: +10% for Iron Hail Gunners, Crane Gunners, and Nan-Gau Grenadiers in her army.

Translation: Miao Ying's army is the gunpowder doomstack of Cathay. Build it that way.

☯Miao Ying, the Storm Dragon

Miao Ying, the Storm Dragon
Favoured child of the Celestial Emperor · Supreme Matriarch · Legendary Lord

"Favoured child of the Celestial Emperor, revered defender of the Great Bastion and imperious majesty incarnate." One of the most flexible Legendary Lords in the game — a Wizard with full spell mastery, a melee combatant in human form, and a flying single-entity dragon when the situation requires it.

Combat profile (Standard form)

Wizard 100% Spell Mastery Armoured Melee Expert Magical Attacks Encourage Hide (forest) Siege Attacker Mastery of Elemental Winds Harmony Amplifier +100% Resilience (Dragon Emperor's Wrath) Single Entity
  • Stats: 8,424 HP, Leadership 90, Speed 46, Melee Attack 65, Melee Defence 55, Charge Bonus 50.
  • Weapon Strength 430 (290 base / 140 AP) with magical attacks.
  • Armour 70 (52.5% reduction) + +15% Missile Resistance + +25% Spell Resistance.
  • Harmony Amplifier +100%: doubles the Harmony bonuses for nearby units. Only the strongest amplifier bonus applies — she is your strongest, so put her central in the line.
  • Mastery of Elemental Winds: when 2+ units in the same army share this attribute, intensity increases the power of spells cast. Pair her with Astromancers / Alchemists / Shugengan to compound it.
  • Active abilities: Transformation of the Dragon, Wrath of the Storm; campaign: Foe-Seeker, Deadly Onslaught, Rally!, Stand Your Ground!
  • Passives: Arcane Conduit, Disdain of the Dragons, Power of Yin, Life Bloom, Wounds; campaign-only Evasion.

Spells & weaknesses

Spell list

  • Earth Blood — AoE healing aura. Heals units, not just lords.
  • Storm of Shadows — AoE damage spell.
  • Missile Mirror — reflects enemy missiles.
  • Flesh to Stone — armour buff on a friendly unit.
  • Regrowth — large heal-over-time on a friendly unit.
  • Talons of Night — damaging projectile spell.

The two ways to lose Miao Ying

  • Massed AP missiles in Dragon form. She is a single entity in either form, but the Dragon's bigger silhouette is a magnet for Sisters of Avelorn, Ratling Guns, Empire Great Cannons, and similar focus-fire setups.
  • Reckless duels with anti-large monsters. Bonus-vs-large hits her hard; great-weapon Slayers, Halberd Chaos Warriors, and Bloodthirsters chunk her quickly. Her Wounds passive saves her once — don't push for a second.
The healing edge: the wiki notes Miao Ying's strategic edge over her playable siblings is access to Life Bloom and Earth Blood — healing-unit abilities that other lords mostly can't replicate. This makes her surprisingly powerful at leading armies of single-entity units (Jet Lions, Jade Lions, Terracotta Sentinels, Celestial Lions) even though she doesn't specifically buff them.

Unique items

  • Storm Wind Coronal — her crown-style item, contributing to the storm theme.
  • Vambraces of Yin — arm-piece with the on-item ability Vambraces of Yin.

Mounts & the Dragon transformation

  • Standard form (foot/horse-style): the default. Caster + melee hybrid. Place near the front line so her Harmony Amplifier and casting both reach the bulk of your units.
  • Dragon form: activated via Transformation of the Dragon. She becomes a flying single-entity dragon — faster, far more devastating in melee, and able to dive over walls or backline targets. Use it when you need a single-target threat removed (enemy lord, war machine, breach point) or when the line is collapsing and needs a hero's intervention.
  • Note: she still casts spells in either form — the transformation doesn't lock you out of her caster kit.

Lord Effects (army-wide)

  • Upkeep: −50% for Missile Infantry units in her army.
  • Recruit rank: +2 for Iron Hail Gunners, Crane Gunners, and Nan-Gau Grenadiers in her army.
  • Missile strength: +10% for Iron Hail Gunners, Crane Gunners, and Nan-Gau Grenadiers in her army.
Build implication: Miao Ying's stack should lean hard into Cathay gunpowder and missile infantry. Iron Hail Gunners and Crane Gunners are objectively a better build in her army than in any other Cathay lord's — the upkeep cut alone lets you field two extra missile slots.

Skill tree priorities (in order)

  1. Spell-school discounts & winds-of-magic regen first. Her caster kit is half her combat value; cheaper, more frequent spells are the highest-impact picks.
  2. Transformation of the Dragon upgrades. Earlier / cheaper / better Dragon form is a meaningful battlefield power-spike.
  3. Lord effect amplifiers — anything that buffs Iron Hail Gunners / Crane Gunners / Nan-Gau Grenadiers further deepens her army's gunline identity.
  4. Yellow combat damage / armour in moderation — she is a Melee Expert, but you don't want to push her into melee solo.
  5. Lightning Strike when it appears — lets you isolate Chaos Warbands before reinforcements join.
  6. Blue line campaign skills — movement, replenishment, post-battle income; valuable but secondary.

How to use her in battle

  1. Deploy her central, slightly behind the front line. Her Harmony Amplifier +100% wants to reach as many Yin/Yang pairs as possible.
  2. Open with healing prep. Earth Blood / Regrowth on the unit you expect to take the worst hit; Flesh to Stone on the anvil.
  3. Damage spells when enemy infantry blobs. Storm of Shadows / Talons of Night on a clumped target.
  4. Transform when the situation justifies it. Backline caster threatening to break the line, breach point in a siege, enemy lord on a single-entity mount — that's when the Dragon comes out.
  5. Don't transform "just because." Standard form keeps her in Mastery-of-Elemental-Winds range, casting and amplifying. Dragon is a tool for specific moments.
  6. Pull her at orange HP if focused fire is on her. Wounds saves once; a permanent loss is real.

☯Faction Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths

  • The Great Bastion. Real defensive infrastructure — well-upgraded gates beat Kurgan Warbands with no field army needed.
  • Best gunpowder in Cathay. Iron Hail Gunners, Crane Gunners, Nan-Gau Grenadiers are all buffed by Miao Ying's lord effects; a missile gunline that runs +20% longer than baseline.
  • Miao Ying herself. Wizard + dragon + Harmony Amplifier + healing access. One of the most flexible LLs in the game.
  • Eight lores of magic. Caster diversity is unmatched outside High Elves; Mastery of Elemental Winds scales magic damage with caster count.
  • Strong economy via Ivory Road caravans. One successful caravan run pays for a Bastion gate upgrade.
  • Wu-Xing Compass & Harmony give you faction-wide levers no other race has.
  • +10 leadership vs. Chaos / Norsca across every army — the matchups the campaign forces.
  • Untainted & −2 corruption. Provinces stay clean automatically.
  • Strong starting position. The wiki: "You start in a reasonably sheltered position."

Weaknesses

  • Slow-moving and easy to flank. The race's defining battlefield weakness; Cathay armies are set-piece machines, not pursuers.
  • Harmony decays if you ignore it. Lazy play tilts the meter; balance takes attention.
  • Caravans can fail. Ivory Road dilemmas can turn a profit run into a destroyed convoy. Always escort with anti-large units.
  • Three serious threats by mid-game. Warhost of Zharr, Puppets of Misrule, and Clan Eshin all want you dead by turn 30.
  • Sky-junk fire damage is wasted on Chaos Dwarfs — their roster resists fire. Bring the Luminark Lens.
  • Vilitch's Changing of the Ways can be a campaign-disrupting headache. Plan around it.
  • The Bastion north territory (Chaos Wastes climate) is "almost completely worthless" — you can't keep what you take across the wall.
  • Defensive philosophy can feel reactive. If you want to crusade, the Western Provinces are the better Cathay choice.

☯Campaign Strategy

Miao Ying's campaign is three phases: secure the Gunpowder Road and Lands of Stone and Steel, lock down the Western/Central/Eastern Bastion provinces, and confederate / destroy the rival Cathay claimants while running scheduled crusades against the named victory targets.

Immortal Empires — the opening ten turns

Starting position: Nan-Gau, Gunpowder Road. Starting wars: Rebel Lords of Nan-Yang, Kurgan Warband. Starting NAP: Imperial Wardens. Starting hero: an Alchemist.
  1. Turn 1: embed your Alchemist starting hero in Miao Ying's army and upgrade Increase Mobility after the first battle. This unlocks the four-turn province sweep below.
  2. Turn 2–5: with the bonus mobility, take each of your starting enemy's settlements one turn after the other. Do not let them build a response. Two complete provinces under your control by turn 4 is the wiki-recommended target.
  3. Watch for the Beastmen tribe south of your position. It may be wise to double back and recruit in Nan-Li or Weng Chang — wait for them to break cover, then wipe them out cleanly.
  4. Activate diplomacy aggressively. Trade with Imperial Wardens, the Western Provinces, and any Cathay minor that will accept it. Caravans on the Ivory Road start funding your war effort.
  5. Stand up Bastion garrisons in your starting provinces. The −20% construction cost on Grand Bastion buildings starts paying off the moment you queue your first upgrade.
  6. By turn ~10 you should have two clean provinces, an Alchemist with Increase Mobility, an upgraded Bastion gate or two, and an Ivory Road caravan en route.

The mid-game pattern

1 mobile Miao Ying army + 1 anchor army + Bastion gate garrisons + caravan tax. Miao Ying flies to threatened gates and crisis points; a second Boyar / Lord-led army anchors a vulnerable secondary front (typically the south, vs. Beastmen / Skaven); each gate garrison absorbs Kurgan raids; one or two caravans run continuously on the Ivory Road for income.

The three serious threats and how to time them

  • Clan Eshin is "likely to wind up at war with a lot of the factions surrounding them, including The Western Provinces." If you feel confident the Bastion can do without you for the time being, pile in on them — remove the rat infestation as quickly as possible.
  • Chaos Dwarfs (Warhost of Zharr) and Tzeentch (Puppets of Misrule) mutually dislike each other. Watch their relations; when they're at war, peace out with one or both, then focus down a single target. Vilitch is usually the right pick because his Changing of the Ways disrupts your campaign far worse than Astragoth's static gunline.
  • The Blessed Dread (Dark Elves) become a problem sooner or later. Confederate the Celestial Loyalists and claim Wei-Jin before they do; engage their main forces out of Black Ark cannon range.

The Sky-junk vs. Chaos Dwarfs gotcha

Much of the Chaos Dwarfs' roster is resistant to fire damage. Sky-junks — your fire-themed flying war machine — under-perform in this matchup. The wiki recommends the Luminark Lens item to compensate. Bring it before any Chaos Dwarf war.

Empire expansion principles

  • Hold the Bastion provinces, sack everything else hostile. Western Bastion, Central Bastion, Eastern Bastion are your long-victory targets.
  • Don't try to keep territory north of the wall. Chaos Wastes climate is "almost completely worthless." Raze everything east of The Volary; Kurgans are Rogue Armies and can't ruin-dwell, so razing actively reduces threat.
  • Use the Threat Reduction edict on each gate. Significantly cuts how often you have to deal with Kurgan raids.
  • Confederate Cathay minor factions when their loyalty is high enough. Imperial Wardens and Celestial Loyalists are the most realistic targets.
  • Don't crusade for fun. Miao Ying is a defender, not Skarbrand — the campaign rewards sitting at the wall.

Endgame & victory

Short Campaign Victory:

  • Occupy/loot/raze/sack 30 different settlements
  • Control these provinces (direct ownership, vassals, or military allies): Gunpowder Road, Lands of Stone and Steel, Imperial Road, Western Great Bastion, Central Great Bastion, Eastern Great Bastion

Long Campaign Victory:

  • Occupy/loot/raze/sack 70 different settlements
  • Control Western, Central, and Eastern Great Bastion provinces
  • Destroy: Rebel Lords of Nan-Yang, Dissenter Lords of Jinshen, Puppets of Misrule, Heralds of the Tempest, The Blessed Dread, Clan Eshin

Realm of Chaos: complete the survival battle for each Chaos Realm and then win the final battle (Forge of Souls).

☯Battle Strategy

Cathay battles are combined-arms set-pieces with Yin/Yang harmony layered on top. Your line holds, your gunpowder kills, your dragons fly, and your casters compound. With Miao Ying specifically, the gunline is buffed, the Harmony Amplifier is doubled, and the Storm Dragon is in the army.

Yin/Yang positioning — the rule

Yin units want a Yang neighbour

Place Crane Gunners directly behind or beside Peasant Long Spearmen. Sky-junks should hover near a Yang anchor on the flank. An isolated Yin unit reloads slower — that's lost DPS.

Yang units want a Yin neighbour

Peasant Long Spearmen behind / next to Crane Gunners gain melee defence. Celestial Dragon Guard fight harder when an Astromancer floats nearby. An isolated Yang unit takes more melee damage — that's a thinner line.

Standard battle plan

  1. Deploy in a Yin/Yang interleaved line. Spearmen front, Crane Gunners directly behind, Celestial Dragon Guard on the wings, Sky-junks central-rear, Grand Cannons elevated.
  2. Open with healing prep. Miao Ying drops Earth Blood / Flesh to Stone on her front-line as the enemy commits to the approach.
  3. Sky-junks alpha-strike the highest-value target in range — usually the enemy lord or their flying / fast-cavalry threat.
  4. Grand Cannons fire on enemy artillery, monsters, or the most expensive single target.
  5. Crane Gunner / Iron Hail Gunner volleys as the enemy enters their range. Buffed reload from Yin/Yang and +10% missile strength from Miao Ying = devastating concentrated fire.
  6. Spearmen and Jade Warriors absorb the charge. +10 leadership vs. Chaos means they hold; By Our Beards-style stability.
  7. Drop damage spells when enemy infantry blobs — Storm of Shadows / Talons of Night on the densest target.
  8. Transform Miao Ying into the Storm Dragon when the moment justifies it — breach point, enemy lord exposed, line collapsing on a flank. Otherwise, keep her in Standard form for amplifier reach.
  9. Sky-junks dive the back line when their alpha-strike role is done — chase routing units, hit enemy artillery.

Specific matchups

vs. Chaos / Norscan stacks

+10 leadership stabilises your line; Iron Hail Gunners shred Chaos Warriors; Miao Ying's Talons of Night snipes the lord. The matchup the faction is built for.

vs. Chaos Dwarfs (Warhost of Zharr)

Sky-junks under-perform — their fire damage is resisted. Bring the Luminark Lens. Lean on Iron Hail Gunners for AP damage and Astromancer / Alchemist spells. Miao Ying snipes their Daemonsmiths.

vs. Tzeentch (Puppets of Misrule / Vilitch)

Spell Resistance on Miao Ying helps personally. Bring two Alchemists / Astromancers for Mastery of Elemental Winds scaling. Snipe Vilitch fast — Changing of the Ways gets worse the longer he lives.

vs. Skaven (Clan Eshin)

Sky-junks dominate Skaven swarms. Crane Gunners volley through clumped slaves. Miao Ying transforms to clear stalking Globadiers and Triads.

vs. Greenskin / Beastmen swarms

Numbers war — Grand Cannons, Sky-junks, Nan-Gau Grenadier AoE. Spearmen anvil; Celestial Dragon Guard counter-flank. Use the Bastion if you can fight on it.

vs. Cavalry-heavy / fast-flanking

Halberd-equipped Jade Warriors anchor; Crane Gunners volley charges; Celestial Dragon Guard counter-charge. Miao Ying transforms to chase Royal Pegasi or fast cavalry lords.

Auto-resolve advice

Miao Ying's stack auto-resolves well against weak-but-numerous foes, because the auto-resolver weights her stats heavily. Always manually fight against Chaos Dwarfs (the fire-resist gotcha), magic-heavy stacks (Tzeentch / High Elves), and any battle where you've stacked Sky-junks heavily — the auto-resolver tends to undervalue the alpha-strike.

☯Heroes & Casters — Management Guide

Cathay's hero economy is caster-heavy by design. Alchemists, Astromancers, Shugengan, Lords, and Miao Ying herself all share Mastery of Elemental Winds, which means more casters in the same army compound spell power up to +100%. If you don't lean on heroes you are leaving the faction's biggest scaling lever unused.

Hero categories — what they're for

Alchemist (Lore of Metal)

Combat-leaning caster hero. Embed in Miao Ying's army turn 1 — their Increase Mobility campaign skill enables the four-turn province sweep that's the recommended opening.

Rule of thumb: always one Alchemist in your main army for the campaign-map mobility, plus the Mastery scaling.

Astromancer (Lore of Heavens)

Long-range damage caster — lightning, comets, weather. Mastery of Elemental Winds caster slot; second hero in any caster-heavy army.

Rule of thumb: embed in armies that face magic-heavy enemies (Tzeentch / High Elves) or whenever you want compounded spell damage.

Dragon-Blooded Shugengan

Specialist caster — Lore of Yin or Lore of Yang. Yin Shugengan emphasise debuffs and ranged support; Yang Shugengan emphasise melee buffs and damage.

Rule of thumb: match the Shugengan's lore to your army composition — Yin army → Yin Shugengan, Yang army → Yang Shugengan.

Lord (combat hero variant)

Pure-melee duelist when you don't want a third caster. Mountable, leadership aura, simpler kit.

Rule of thumb: swap in a combat Lord if your army is already at 3+ casters and the Mastery cap is approaching.

How to manage your hero roster

  1. Embed an Alchemist in Miao Ying's army on turn 1. The Increase Mobility skill is the campaign opening.
  2. Recruit a second caster (Astromancer) by turn 5. Mastery of Elemental Winds at +15% kicks in immediately; Miao Ying's already a caster, so the army runs at +15% from the second caster onward.
  3. Stack 3–4 casters for major battles. A "magic-heavy push army" with Miao Ying + 3 caster heroes runs at +45%, which makes Storm of Shadows / Talons of Night / Comet of Cassandora genuinely battle-deciding.
  4. Use a Lord (combat) for secondary armies. They lead Bastion garrisons or Cathay-minor confederation armies cheaply.
  5. Use heroes as campaign-map agents. Wound Enemy Hero, Block Army, Embed for buffs — particularly against Vilitch and Chaos Dwarf Daemonsmiths.
  6. Don't keep idle heroes in cities for more than 3 turns. Hero upkeep adds up; either embed them, send them as agents, or disband.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the Alchemist's mobility upgrade. The opening four-turn sweep depends on it; without it the campaign opens slower and harder.
  • Putting all your casters in one army. Mastery of Elemental Winds caps at +100%; stuffing 8 casters wastes the surplus. Spread them — one main army at +60%, a second at +30%.
  • Mismatching Shugengan lore to army. A Yin Shugengan in a Yang melee stack is a wasted hero slot.
  • Letting Miao Ying solo cast without amplifier reach. Her Harmony Amplifier +100% is a battlefield buff — she belongs near Yin/Yang pairs, not isolated.
  • Forgetting to use Wound Enemy Hero on Vilitch. Disrupting Changing of the Ways with a hero action is one of the best campaign-map plays available.

☯The Great Bastion — Protecting the Gates

The Great Bastion is the headline mechanic of the Northern Provinces. Each gate is its own piece of defensive infrastructure with garrisons, threat tracking, and upgrade chains. Well-upgraded gates beat Kurgan Warbands without your field army even showing up. Manage them properly and the Bastion is a passive engine of attrition for every Chaos warband that drifts south from the Eastern Steppes.

The Bastion at a glance

  • The Bastion stretches across three Bastion provinces: Western Great Bastion, Central Great Bastion, Eastern Great Bastion — all three are required for victory.
  • Each gate fortress is a siege-ready settlement with its own garrison, walls, and threat bar.
  • Threat bars rise as Chaos / Kurgan armies approach from the north; when high, a Kurgan Warband spawns and assaults.
  • Upgrading gates costs gold + Defensive Supplies; your faction effects discount this by 20%, and Defensive Supplies arrive at +300/region.

The Threat Reduction edict

Use the Threat Reduction edict on every gate. The wiki states this "will significantly reduce how often you have to deal with" Kurgan Warbands. It's the single best edict choice on a Bastion province; turn it on and leave it on.

Gate upgrade priority

  1. Walls / fortification chain first. Upgraded walls let the gate beat assaulting Warbands by garrison alone.
  2. Garrison-quality chain second. Better garrison units mean even unfortunate matchups (e.g., Mammoth-heavy stacks) don't break the gate.
  3. Defensive Supplies stockpile / regional supply chain. Compounds your faction's +300 Defensive Supplies effect.
  4. Threat Reduction-supporting buildings. Buildings or commandments that cut threat accumulation directly.
  5. Income / commodity buildings only when the gate is otherwise stable. Don't make money at the cost of a gate falling.

Field army & gate coverage

  • Miao Ying's army should rotate between gates. Don't let her camp at one — she's the response force for whichever threat bar climbs fastest.
  • A second army covers gates Miao Ying isn't standing at. Boyar / Lord-led, missile-heavy, cheaper to maintain.
  • When threat bars are quiet, use the spare turns for Ivory Road caravans or aggressive minor-Cathay confederations.
  • Lightning Strike on Miao Ying is critical when a Kurgan Warband approaches with reinforcements — isolate them, kill them, return to the gate.

The "north of the wall" question

Do not try to hold territory north of the Bastion. The wiki is explicit: it's Chaos Wastes climate, "almost completely worthless" to you, and Kurgan Warbands are Rogue Armies — they can't ruin-dwell, so razing actively reduces the threat. Raze everything east of The Volary; it's better as scorched earth than as your problem.

If a gate falls

It happens. The wiki even acknowledges the Bastion has been breached on rare occasions. When a gate falls:
  • Re-take immediately, in the same turn if possible. A retaken gate is a settled grudge and a piece of infrastructure recovered.
  • Don't chase the Warband north. They lure you into Chaos Wastes climate and pile in with reinforcements. Let them spend their attack against your line on the way back.
  • Re-build with priority on walls and garrison. Income chains can wait.
  • Use Miao Ying's Dragon form on the retake assault — siege battles favour the dragon's flying mobility and breach-point firepower.

The Bastion as long-victory infrastructure

The Long Campaign Victory requires you to control the three Bastion provinces (Western, Central, Eastern). Treat the Bastion not as a chore but as the campaign itself — everything else (Ivory Road income, confederations, hostile-faction destruction) funds and protects the wall.

☯Raze, Sack, or Occupy?

Cathay plays standard settlement rules — Loot/Sack/Raze/Occupy. The Northern Provinces twist is that territory north of the Bastion is worse than worthless (Chaos Wastes climate), while every Bastion province is mandatory for the long victory.

The three actions and what they do

Sack

Beat the garrison, take a one-time gold payout, do not occupy, the city stays where it was.

  • Modest XP and post-battle income
  • Owner survives; can be re-sacked later
  • Useful when you can't justify defending
Use vs. distant Empire / Bretonnia / Steppe cities you don't want to keep.

Raze

Burn the settlement to ruins. The province becomes empty.

  • Removes corruption from the region
  • Denies the enemy a rebuild target
  • Specifically against Kurgan Rogue Armies, razing reduces threat
Use vs. all territory north of the Bastion. Raze everything east of The Volary.

Loot & Occupy

Sack and take the city in one action. Default for Cathay-suitable terrain.

  • You now own the settlement — garrison, building chain, all income
  • Public order takes a hit; standard fixes apply
  • Bastion-relevant settlements unlock gate upgrade chains
Default action for any Cathay-climate settlement, especially Bastion provinces.

The decision rule (memorize this)

Climate first, Bastion second, Grudges (caravans) third.
  • If it's a Bastion province settlement → Loot & Occupy, always. These are victory targets.
  • If it's core Cathay climate → Loot & Occupy.
  • If it's Habitable but exposed (you can't reinforce it) → Sack for the gold, then walk away.
  • If it's north of the Bastion (Chaos Wastes climate) → Raze. Always. It's worthless to you and burning it cuts threat.
  • If it belongs to a victory-target faction you must destroy — Rebel Lords, Dissenter Lords, Heralds of the Tempest, Blessed Dread, Clan Eshin → whatever you do, destroy the faction.

The Volary line — raze everything east of it

The wiki strategy explicitly states: raze everything east of The Volary. The reasoning is that this territory is Chaos Wastes climate (uninhabitable / unpleasant for Cathay) and Kurgan Warbands are Rogue Armies that cannot occupy ruins. Razing actively cuts their habitat and threat. Don't be tempted by gold — it's a campaign-positive action even if it feels destructive.

Which provinces should you actually keep?

  • Gunpowder Road (your starting province around Nan-Gau) — always.
  • Lands of Stone and Steel — short-victory target.
  • Imperial Road — short-victory target.
  • Western, Central, and Eastern Great Bastion — mandatory for long victory.
  • Wei-Jin region — claim before The Blessed Dread does.
  • Avoid territory north of the Bastion. Chaos Wastes climate is "almost completely worthless."
  • Avoid distant Habitable cities you can't reinforce — sack for gold, don't keep.
Climate trap: Loot & Occupy a city in Uninhabitable climate and you bleed public order, money, and growth permanently. Read the climate icon before you click. Cathay-suitable climates only — the Bastion is your wall, not your border.

☯Diplomacy & Economy

Cathay has full diplomacy, real trade, and a rich economy fed by the Ivory Road. The Northern Provinces specifically benefit from Bastion Defender / Caravan Master reputation traits and the unique Ivory Road caravan income system.

Diplomacy: actually useful

  • Starting NAP with Imperial Wardens. Don't break it; upgrade to trade and defensive alliance as relations improve.
  • Trade with the Western Provinces and Jade Court as soon as relations allow. Inter-Cathay trade is the strongest commodity flow available to you.
  • Confederate Cathay minor factions. Imperial Wardens and Celestial Loyalists are realistic candidates — aim to claim the Celestial Loyalists and Wei-Jin before The Blessed Dread does.
  • Trade with the Empire / Bretonnia / Dwarfs / High Elves via the Ivory Road. Their gold flows back to you while you defend the Bastion.
  • Don't ally with Chaos / Skaven / Vampires / Dark Elves. Reputation hit propagates to every Order partner you also want.
  • Diplomatic traits to expect: other factions read you as Protective, Bastion Defender, Caravan Master. Order factions like working with you; Chaos factions hate you on principle.
  • Don't waste diplomacy on the three named threats. Warhost of Zharr, Puppets of Misrule, and Clan Eshin will fight you anyway — play them off each other instead.
Practical rule: open the diplomacy screen every turn. Look for new trade partners, friendly-territory access offers, and Cathay-minor confederation triggers. There is almost always a free move available.

The Ivory Road — how caravans actually work

  1. Stake a caravan from a Cathay city heading west to the Old World. The caravan is led by a Caravan Master with a small accompanying army.
  2. Equip the caravan with sturdy infantry and anti-large units. Ogres are the headline threat — an Ogre Lord ambush ends a poorly-equipped caravan.
  3. Watch for dilemmas en route. The Ivory Road throws random events that may enhance the rewards or destroy the convoy. Choose carefully.
  4. Defeating an Ogre Lord during a caravan run grants meaningful loot, on top of the destination payout.
  5. Run caravans continuously. One in flight at all times; income compounds.

Economy: how you actually make money

Cathay's treasury is fed by buildings, trade, Ivory Road caravans, and Defensive Supplies, supported by battle income. Sources in order of importance:

  1. Ivory Road caravans. A successful run pays for a Bastion gate upgrade.
  2. Trade deals. Bilateral trade with Cathay, Empire, Bretonnia, Dwarfs, High Elves. Five trade partners and you're rich.
  3. Building income. Tier-3+ commodity buildings, ports, and growth chains stack income.
  4. Battle / sack income. Real but secondary. Don't structure your campaign around it.
  5. Wu-Xing Compass economic buffs. When the Compass is pointed at an income setting, faction-wide income spikes.

Building priorities for the cities you keep

  • Tier-up the capital recruitment chain first — Crane Gunner / Iron Hail Gunner / Sky-junk access fast.
  • Bastion gate upgrades on every Bastion-province settlement — the −20% construction cost compounds.
  • Trade resource chains in interior provinces — silk, jade, porcelain commodities.
  • Caravan infrastructure in cities with Ivory Road access — cheaper / faster caravans = compounding income.
  • Skip generic income buildings in favour of higher-yield commodity chains.

Cash-flow troubleshooting

If your treasury is bleeding red:
  • Run a caravan immediately. Most reliable Cathay income source.
  • Renegotiate trade deals upward as your trade-good output grows.
  • Disband a reserve army. Cathay recruitment is cheap-but-tier-gated; you can rebuild.
  • Sack a southern lowland city — you weren't going to keep it anyway.
  • Point the Wu-Xing Compass at an economic buff for a faction-wide income window.

Public order & corruption

  • Faction effect −2 corruption + Cathay Untainted default = corruption rarely a problem.
  • Public order in newly-occupied cities is helped by standard happiness chains and a garrisoned Lord.
  • Harmony tilt can affect public order indirectly — balanced Harmony stabilises mood.
  • If a settlement does revolt, retake it the same turn for free XP, gold, and a clean restart.
